Liangliang Xiang

Postdoctoral Research Fellow

Department of Engineering Mechanics, KTH Royal Institute of Technology

I am a Postdoctoral Research Fellow at the Department of Engineering Mechanics, KTH Royal Institute of Technology, working at the intersection of wearable sensing, artificial intelligence, and human movement biomechanics. My research develops data-driven frameworks, combining musculoskeletal modelling, deep learning, and wearable sensors, to enable precision rehabilitation and human performance enhancement.

I currently serve as Principal Investigator on two funded projects totalling over 2.6 million SEK: one on data-driven gait biomechanics for precision rehabilitation (Digital Futures / RISE, 2025–2027), and one on a tibia digital twin for runners using smartphone scans and wearable-AI prediction (Swedish Research Council for Sports Science, 2026–2027). I have authored 40+ SCI-indexed publications (15+ as first or corresponding author), including ESI Highly Cited works, appearing in journals such as npj Digital Medicine, Journal of Biomechanics, and Computers in Biology and Medicine.

I received my Ph.D. in Bioengineering from the University of Auckland (2024) and my M.Ed. in Biomechanics from Ningbo University (2020).

Research

Data-Driven Gait Biomechanics for Precision Rehabilitation

PI-led Digital Futures / RISE project (2,000,000 SEK, 2025–2027) developing wearable-AI pipelines that translate real-world gait data into personalised rehabilitation targets for patients with neurological and musculoskeletal conditions.

Precision RehabilitationWearable AIGait AnalysisDigital Futures

Tibia Digital Twin for Runners

PI-led CIF grant project (614,000 SEK, 2026–2027) building a full pipeline from smartphone-based photogrammetric foot scanning to personalised finite-element tibial models combined with wearable-AI to predict bone stress and injury risk in recreational runners.

Digital TwinSmartphone ScanningRunning InjuryBone StressCIF

Deep Learning for Bone Stress Prediction

A domain-adaptation LSTM framework for real-time biomechanical inference of tibial bone stress from wearable sensor data, removing the need for laboratory-grade equipment. Published in npj Digital Medicine (2025).

Deep LearningLSTMBone StressWearablesOpen Source

Deep Reinforcement Learning for Muscle Redundancy

A DRL framework that solves the muscle redundancy problem in real time, enabling continuous inference of individual muscle forces from a musculoskeletal model without the computational cost of static optimisation.

Reinforcement LearningMusculoskeletal ModellingMuscle SynergiesOpen Source

Statistical Shape Models of the Human Foot

Open-access population-based statistical shape models of the foot and ankle, enabling personalised 3D foot-ankle modelling from low-cost scans. Dataset hosted on Zenodo, used in multiple downstream biomechanics studies.

Statistical Shape ModellingFoot BiomechanicsOpen Data
